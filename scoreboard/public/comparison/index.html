How to read the ratings
KBB owner rating (x.x/5, n shown)
Self-selected owner reviews on kbb.com — people motivated to rate. Measures satisfaction among owners who bothered to review; small n (under ~50) means noise. KBB pools one rating set per generation, not per model-year.
U.S. News overall (x.x/10)
An aggregation of published critic reviews plus safety and reliability data — no owner input. Good proxy for what professional reviewers concluded; lags current model years.
U.S. News reliability (0–100) = J.D. Power Predicted Reliability
Derived from J.D. Power owner surveys: problems per 100 vehicles at 90 days and at 3 years of ownership, transformed to a 0–100 scale. 80+ is great, the 70s are average, below 70 is below average. Predicts, not measures, this exact model-year.
RepairPal average annual repair cost ($/yr)
Computed from repair invoices submitted to RepairPal network shops — actual money spent, not opinions. Skews toward cars whose owners use independent shops; the CX-90 is too new for model-level data, so the make-level Mazda figure is shown and flagged — read it as an approximation.
NHTSA recalls / complaints (counts)
Recalls are manufacturer-initiated or NHTSA-ordered safety campaigns — objective counts. Complaints are owner-submitted and unverified; count scales with sales volume, so compare against the recalls column and sample sizes, not across makes blindly.
Edmunds expert + consumer ratings
Not available in this build: Edmunds serves an Akamai bot-challenge to our fetcher (recorded in data/edmunds.json), so rather than guess, we show nothing. KBB owner ratings and U.S. News/J.D. Power cover the same ground.

So which one?

Honda Pilot 2021–22. The value captain's play: 2021 Touring/Elite FMPs landed in the low-to-mid $20s KBB and real AWD listings cluster at $29–31k with 7-passenger captain's builds findable (see examples marked 'Rear Captain's Chairs'). Oldest cabin tech here; 5,000-lb tow with AWD.

Mazda CX-9 2023. The no-drama pick: every trim AWD, leather + captain's chairs standard from the base Touring — the cheapest guaranteed-pass on the no-bench rule ($30k median, 22 samples). Trade-offs: smallest of the five, 3,500-lb tow, roof rails optional.

The roomy upgrade: AWD-only, 5,000-lb tow from Preferred Plus up, 12.3-inch wireless-CarPlay cabin, and the biggest used discount of the group ($30.6k median on 132 samples for a ~$46k-trim-rung car). Captain's needs the upper trims; first-model-year reliability not yet rated.

GMC Acadia 2023. The cheap seat: $30.9k median across 210 samples, 4,000-lb tow, captain's optional on SLT/AT4/Denali (std bench default — check the build). Last year of the smaller gen-2 body; RepairPal cost is the highest of the group ($734/yr).

If the no-bench rule is absolute and the budget is ~$30k: the CX-9 Touring (captain's + AWD + leather standard) and a captain's-equipped CX-90 Premium Plus / S Premium are the surest paths; a Pilot EX-L/Touring or TrailSport with the 7-seat layout is the value path if you can verify the build.
